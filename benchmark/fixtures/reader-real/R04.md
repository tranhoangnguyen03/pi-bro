Yes, it's relevant, but in a narrow way. It's worth borrowing two or three rules and one measurement idea. It's not worth adopting the skill itself.

**Why only partly.** SimpleEnglish sets the style an agent uses for its own writing, like docs and chat replies. Bro does a different job: it rewrites someone else's dense text for a tired reader, and it deliberately keeps the source's formatting, code and warnings. Their core rule, "prose only: no headers, bullets, bold or tables," would break our `balanced` and `faithful` modes.

**Their best finding backs our current approach.** Their own audit (`evals/results/WHY-USELESS-2026-09-02.md`) found that 50+ rules drowned out the 5 that matter. An 8-line prompt beat their full skill on everything a reader actually sees. Our prompts already work that way: they describe the reader and let the model decide the form (`prompt.ts:103`). So we should stay short and not import their rulebook.

**Worth adopting:**

1. **Concrete rules instead of adjectives.** Their FAQ makes the point: "'Clearly' is an opinion. 'No sentence over 20 words' is a spec." Our mode prompts mostly use adjectives like "plain, simpleton-friendly language" (`prompt.ts:20-24`). Three rules fit Bro's job directly:
   - **Define jargon in a few words the first time it appears**, e.g. "idempotent (safe to run twice)". This is the most useful one for explaining dense AI replies, and none of our modes asks for it today.
   - **Put the condition before the command:** "If the build fails, read the log." That way the reader doesn't act before reaching the "...only if X" at the end. It fits `balanced` well, since that mode already promises to keep conditions.
   - **State the fact, not how important it is.** Cut words like "crucial," "robust" and "seamlessly." This sharpens "trim fluff."
2. **Count what the reader sees in the benchmark.** Our mechanical checks cover missing literals, preambles and length. Adding counts for words, sentences, em-dashes, bold, headers and bullets per output is cheap. These counts are most meaningful for `brief` and BTW, where we don't promise to keep formatting.
3. **Watch for their loophole.** Their length cap said list items don't count, so the model put everything in bullets. If we ever add a length cap to `brief` or BTW, list items must count.

A smaller point: our own prompt text uses a lot of em-dashes. It's widely believed, though I haven't seen it measured, that models copy the style of their prompt. That's cheap to clean up if we touch these prompts anyway.

**Not worth adopting:** the strict STE vocabulary, the "no contractions" rule (BTW's friendly tone is intentional), the prose-only reply rule for explain output, and their hook/plugin packaging.

**One caveat:** all their numbers come from Claude Sonnet 4.6, with Claude models as judges. Our default backend is Agy (Google), so any change needs our own benchmark run before we trust it.
